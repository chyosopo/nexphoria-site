## What the site does (read from the rendered copy)
- Hero: "Personalised vitamins made for your (energy/sleep/beauty/focus/calm)".
  The bracketed word rotates every two seconds, in the accent colour, with a
  fade. One line under it, one button: "Start Quiz".
- Three steps with verb headings: "Take your quiz" / "Get personalised" /
  "Refill & replenish", one sentence each, second person, everyday words
  ("It only takes a few mins").
- "Vitamins as unique as you": three example people, each with goals and the
  formula built for them.
- "Why ALYVE": four plain tiles. Founder's story in first person. FAQ in the
  same warm register. A quiz ("What vitamins do I need?") as the front door
  of the whole site; the product pages are secondary.
- Product pages: one line, benefits as short bullets, how to take it, FAQ.

## What we took
- The rotating word in the hero: "Peptide therapy, prescribed for your
  [sleep / focus / recovery / weight / energy / skin / desire]." The first
  word renders statically; reduced motion sees a still sentence.
- A quiz as a front door: /quiz, four preference questions (what you are
  treating, how you would rather take it, how soon you want to notice
  something, one medicine or a plan) ending at the medicines a physician can
  prescribe for the goal, then the health questions. Nothing is stored or
  sent; no health question is asked on our side.
- Second person and everyday words in the steps and the quiz. Verb-first step
  headings.

## What we did not take, and why
- Testimonials with names and towns, "UK's highest-rated", "100,000+",
  Trustpilot, "As seen in": we do not publish testimonials or outcomes.
- "No more guesswork. No more handfuls of pills.": defensive negation, law 3.
- 25% off, free gifts, 60-day money-back guarantee, "70% less than": no
  discounts, no guarantees, no comparison; we inform, we do not persuade.
- "Big Nutra", the founder's medication story: nothing about the market, and
  no health claims about a person.
- Emoji bullets and exclamation marks.
